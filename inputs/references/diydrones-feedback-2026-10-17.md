# r/diydrones feedback on PETGasus v0.1 (2026-10-17)

From the thread "PETGasus: Affordable DIY 3D Printable Drone" on r/diydrones, read
from a screenshot the author took about ten hours after posting (4K views, 12
comments). Paraphrased; the commenters are not named. The author replied that the
design will be revised.

1. The ducts are too tall. They will cause propwash in climbs, rolls and pitch
   changes. Keep a guard to 4 mm tall or less and spend the mass where it carries load.
2. The triangular cutouts in the ducts will make strange propwash. Remove them.
   (Another commenter: the cutouts also ADD weight, because each adds a lot of
   layer perimeter.)
3. Too many right angles and flat plank pieces. Rectangular, un-gusseted flat
   shapes copy carbon-fibre plate and carry none of its strength.
4. Printed PETG is 15 to 50 MPa in tension, depending on infill and other
   settings; even cheap carbon-fibre plate is about 2100 MPa.
5. Flat faces butt against each other with no gussets and no triangulation. The
   small joints between them need something to give them a chance.
6. If it were FPV it would need a canopy, preferably not flat. (It is
   line-of-sight.)
7. It will fly; the questions are durability and how well. A couple of crashes
   will almost certainly break the frame: PETG-CF is too stiff and brittle, plain
   PETG too flexible and weak.
8. The motor, board and other values the design marks "unconfirmed" are published.
9. Where the outer prop shroud meets the cross member there is a large stress
   concentrator on top, and layers running continuously round the top make that
   region very rigid. Fillet horizontally, chamfer vertically.
10. Make filament choice central to the design: cheap printers now run abrasive,
    high-temperature filaments for more rigidity. Look for places to run
    pultruded carbon rods and flats through the structure.
11. "3D printer but it's still a 2D design."
12. "I have never seen a 3D printed whoop frame. It looks goofy. It would work
    better like a toothpick." A third commenter disagreed with the propwash
    point: half-ducted whoops (AOS Cine25 Evo) fly well, and right angles and
    flat planks are the real weakness, because PETG breaks easily.
