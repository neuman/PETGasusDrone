PETGasus Grounding Map

PETGasus · grounding map

Design checks for a small 3D-printed drone

What is this page?

The drone. PETGasus is a small drone with ducted propellers. It has one frame, 3D-printed in PETG plastic. The flight board, motors and propellers come from a kit that you buy. You do not have to solder.

The checks. nopekit is the check tool for the design. The design model calculates values such as weight and thrust. Each check compares one value with a limit. A requirement passes when all its checks pass.

The stages. The project has two stages, “Order the parts” and then “Print the frame”. A stage is blocked until all its requirements pass and the owner accepts its estimates. An estimate is a value that nobody measured yet.

Order the parts: BLOCKED

  1. The parts cost $167.15. The limit is $150. Remove $17.15 of parts, or increase the limit.
  2. Four values are estimates. Nobody can measure them before the parts arrive. You must accept them before you order.

Print the frame: BLOCKED. When the kit arrives, measure the flight board and one motor.

The screen is narrow. This view shows the map as lists.

Each line on the map is one requirement. A line goes from the choices (left) through their implications (middle) to its gate (right). Move the pointer onto a line to show it. Click an item to see the details.

Design sources. The map uses ideas from the London Underground diagram by Harry Beck and the bullet graphs by Stephen Few. It also uses ideas from Charles Minard, John Snow, Bret Victor, and the asset checks in Dagster and dbt. The status words come from Table 1 of the paper “Grounding by falsification”.
