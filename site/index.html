PETGasus

PETGasus is a small quadcopter. A low guard protects the outer side of each propeller. You print the frame on a 3D printer. A computer calculated its shape: the plastic goes where the loads from flying and crashing go. The other parts connect with plugs and screws. No soldering is necessary.

Frame Design

Version 0.1 had a flat plate and tall ducts. Experienced builders said that it was "still a 2D design", with flat planks, right angles and weak joints. In version 0.2, a topology optimizer calculated the shape in 3D. Its inputs were the motor seats, the guards on the outer side of each propeller, the flight controller, the battery, the spaces that must stay empty, and the loads from thrust and from crashes. It put the plastic where the loads go, and it joined the four guards into one rim. Each joint has a fillet.

Filament comparison

The same frame shape in each filament. CalculiX calculated the highest drop and the fastest wall hit that each filament survives. A green bar is at the limit or above it. PETG is for everyday prints. Nylon-CF is for strength, and the crash checks use it. Some filaments need more equipment on the Snapmaker U1.
