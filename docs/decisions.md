## 2026-10-18 — Thin walls thickened before the drawing is opened

`thin-walls-thickened-before-the-drawing-is-opene`

The drawing grows every section thinner than its opening keeps at every grid phase (1.75 mm on its 0.25 mm grid) where it can, with balls 1.7 mm across, then opens at 0.65 mm (surface_open_mm: half of C7's 1.26 mm wall and a hair over), sparing the 1.4 mm hub (geometry._thicken, geometry._open). It leaves the exact features' own sections alone: drawn() puts them back at their size. Over the props' discs it grows outward only within the plan the frame already covers there, and up and down where a ball does not fit, so it adds nothing to the shadow (14.6 %; C14 allows 15 %); nowhere does it grow further than 1 mm from the body as first drawn. What it grows are the sheets the field's grey top cell layer draws, 0.35 mm where they join the front and back limbs to the rim, and those the props' keep-out leaves of the layer under it, 0.47-0.61 mm under the guard arcs. On cad.wall_thickness's own ray from every face, all but 32 of about 320,000 faces read at least 1.26 mm: 8 slanted facets at the bottom edge of each seat's centre hole read 0.99 mm (the seat is 1.3 mm between its holes), 8 at the bottom corners of the guard arcs' ends 1.05-1.07 mm (the arc is 1.4 mm), 12 under the guard arcs 1.12-1.23 mm, and 4 at the hub's bottom edge by the side posts 1.19 mm. The gate's 4000 evenly spaced faces read 1.33 mm and miss them; a rebuild that moves its sample has about one chance in three of landing on one. The guards read 4.15 mm tall, as before (C13). The PETG frame is 17.03 g against 16.87 g before. The opening also takes about 480 mm^3 of the body as first drawn, in some 5,900 skims and fringes of at most 20 mm^3 (the largest are the field's fillets along the side bands).

**Rejected**

- **open at 0.63-0.7 mm with nothing thickened first** — it deleted the rim's bridges and cut limbs off the rim, and still left walls of 0.05-0.47 mm where the exact features went back in — infeasible
- **thicken after an opening at 0.3 mm** — the opening had punched pinholes through the 0.35 mm sheets, and their rims stayed 0.5-0.6 mm (C7) — infeasible
- **grow freely over the props' discs** — the sheets under the guard arcs' ends spread into the discs: the shadow went from 14.8 % to 15.06 %, over C14's 15 % — infeasible
- **grow with no reach limit** — measured only when the thickening ran after a 0.3 mm opening: a ball grown against a keep-out's wall left a new thin edge for the next pass, and fins 0.57 mm thick climbed 3.5 mm up each motor's keep-out (45.5 mm^2 of walls under 1.26 mm, against 31 with a 1 mm reach) — unevaluated · returns if (not machine-checkable) "a run in the adopted order, with no reach limit, that leaves no fins"
- **a final opening of 0.6 mm** — 2 x 0.6 = 1.2 mm is under 1.26: it left 1.3 mm^2 of walls at 1.14-1.25 mm where it rounded edges (C7), on a drawing whose sections under 1.46 mm were grown to 1.56 — infeasible
- **square the corner facets with mesh booleans after the level set** — booleans between the field's surface and exact solids left 0.01 mm skins and pinched vertices before (geometry.py's notes); the facets are the level set rounding sharp edges by under half an edge — unevaluated · returns if (not machine-checkable) "a boolean that squares the corners without leaving skins"
- **solve the optimiser again so its top and bottom layers come out solid or empty** — about 45 minutes a run, and no change to the problem is yet known to make the grey layer come out solid or empty (topopt3d: known limitations, 5) — unevaluated · returns if (not machine-checkable) "an optimiser run whose field has no grey top and bottom layers"
- **look for sections only under 1.55 mm (twice the opening plus a step)** — the opening keeps a section at every grid phase only from 1.75 mm: sections drawn 1.55-1.75 mm across were not grown and could be opened away (on test sheets 1.56-1.74 mm thick the opening left nothing) — infeasible
- **thicken the exact features' own sections too** — with the fillet the guard bands are 1.75 mm across at their foot, under the 1.77 mm looked for: grown there, their balls reached 0.65 mm under the bands, and the guards' printed height went from 4.15 mm to 4.6, over C13's 4.5 — infeasible
- **grow nothing under the guards (within 0-0.5 mm of their plan)** — the sheets under the guard arcs run into that zone: cut short there, they left walls of 0.26-0.69 mm (C7) — infeasible

## 2026-10-18 — PETG for everyday prints, PA6-CF for strong ones

`petg-for-everyday-prints-pa6-cf-for-strong-ones`

The frame prints in either filament. PETG is the everyday one: it needs nothing extra on the U1, and on this frame it weighs 16.2 g, its first mode is 143 Hz, and it survives a 2.04 m drop (C15 asks 1 m) and a 1.67 m/s wall hit. The crash claims C15 and C16 are judged in PA6-CF (strong_material), the filament for anyone serious about strength: 13.8 g, 196 Hz, 8.12 m and 3.66 m/s. Of the seven filaments CalculiX checked on the same shape, only it and PAHT-CF clear both crash floors. The owner's brief: most people print PETG casually, nylon is for hardcore builders, and PETG breakability is not a worry.

**Rejected**

- **PAHT-CF as the strong filament** — clears both floors too (7.76 m, 3.44 m/s, 193 Hz, 13.4 g) but by less, with the same drying, hardened nozzle and top cover — dominated · source: input material-comparison
- **PETG-CF** — weaker than plain PETG on this frame (1.62 m, 1.61 m/s, 146 Hz) and needs a hardened nozzle — dominated · source: input material-comparison
- **PLA** — the weakest in a crash (1.57 m, 1.46 m/s) to save half a gram (15.7 g), and it softens in a hot car — dominated · source: input material-comparison
- **PETG with TPU95A guard arcs** — the soft arcs lower the first mode to 136 Hz and gain nothing in a crash (1.94 m, 1.61 m/s) — dominated · source: input material-comparison
- **ASA as the everyday filament** — stiffer (176 Hz) and lighter (13.3 g) with about PETG's crash numbers (1.97 m, 1.61 m/s), but it needs the U1's top cover and gives off styrene: the everyday filament needs nothing extra — preferred against, naming Eric Neuman — nopekit why petg-for-everyday-prints-pa6-cf-for-strong-ones says whether they recorded it · source: input material-comparison

## 2026-10-18 — Crash bars restored to 1 m and 3 m/s

`crash-bars-restored-to-1-m-and-3-m-s`

C15 (drop onto a guard, 1 m) and C16 (wall hit, 3 m/s) are judged in the strong filament (nylon-CF, strong_material) by CalculiX on the free drone, an elastic floor. The author had lowered them to 0.3 m and 1 m/s on numbers a 128-agent review then traced to the gate: CalculiX's multithreaded solver returning solutions out of equilibrium round sliver tets, single nodes at razor-sharp keep-out corners deciding the verdict, an analysis surface thinner than the print, and a drop pose in which the guard could not land first. ## 2026-10-17 — Shadow on the props capped at 15 %; crash checks are an elastic floor

`shadow-on-the-props-capped-at-15-crash-checks-ar`

Each motor seat sits in the middle of its prop's disc, so whatever holds it stiffly shadows the disc. The optimiser charges each cell under a prop 1 + (topo_prop_cost - 1)/N, N the designable cells in its column (topo_prop_cost 10): the extra is shared down the column, so a deep web pays it about once, though a shallow member under-pays (a charge per volume, not a true shadow: model/topopt3d.py's known limitation 2) and C14 allows 15 % of each disc, which costs about 7.5 % of static thrust (A1, guard_thrust_factor 0.925). CalculiX's crash checks are an elastic energy method on the free drone that ignores plastic work, the floor's give and damping: a floor, not a prediction; real survival is P1's drops and wall hits. (Their bars, lowered here to 0.3 m and 1 m/s on a broken gate's numbers, were restored to 1 m and 3 m/s: decisions/crash-bars-restored-to-1-m-and-3-m-s.json.)

**Rejected**

- **a 10 % shadow limit (C14 as it was)** — met only by giving up stiffness: the organic frame held to 8.5 % was 2.9x less stiff than the 22 % one; the author chose the middle (2026-10-17) — preferred against, naming Eric Neuman — nopekit why shadow-on-the-props-capped-at-15-crash-checks-ar says whether they recorded it
- **no limit beyond the cost: the stiffest organic frame** — 22 % of each disc, roughly 11 % of static thrust — preferred against, naming Eric Neuman — nopekit why shadow-on-the-props-capped-at-15-crash-checks-ar says whether they recorded it
- **thin deep blades (a filter 3 mm across, 6 mm deep)** — 6 % shadow at 1.6x A's compliance, but on the 2 mm grid it drew grid-aligned bars round a square hub, the look the redesign set out to leave, in 3 pieces — dominated
- **TPU 95A guard arcs as bumpers** — by the first, held-at-posts crash method they made the crash floors worse (drop 0.084 against 0.113 m, wall 0.42 against 0.78 m/s): the weak joint stayed where the rim's V members meet a side post. The free-body method that replaced it also read the drop floor lower with TPU guards, in its first filament comparison. A linear model is a poor judge of TPU — dominated

**Claims:** `C14`, `A1`, `C15`, `C16`, `P1`

## 2026-10-17 — The frame is drawn from a 3D topology field

`the-frame-is-drawn-from-a-3d-topology-field`

The frame is no longer a plate with spokes. A 3D SIMP optimiser (the solid-fea pack's fea.topopt3d) finds where a 12 g cost-weighted budget of plastic (about 10.7 g placed) carries the loads between the motor seats, the guard arcs and the FC posts: thrust, two prop strikes, nine guard crashes from across the arc in 3D, and the battery. It is solved on a 2 mm grid (5 mm filter) and refined on a 1.33 mm one (3.5 mm filter), with 12 mm of depth free under the seats and material under a prop charged extra, the extra shared down its column. The model draws the refined field (topology_field) as one smooth function, cut, opened and filleted, and one level set. CalculiX on that drawn surface, not the optimiser, judges it (C4, C15, C16); C17 refuses a field solved for another problem.

**Rejected**

- **the v0.1 plate: ribbed spokes and webs from a 2.5D optimiser, flat at the bed** — the r/diydrones review: a 2D design of flat planks meeting at right angles with no gussets, the corners where printed PETG cracks, and expected to break after a couple of crashes — dominated · source: input diydrones-feedback-2026-10-17
- **carbon rods or tubes joined by printed nodes** — the author chose a printed-only frame in the interview (2026-10-17): no cut rods for a kid to build, no second supply — preferred against, naming Eric Neuman — nopekit why the-frame-is-drawn-from-a-3d-topology-field says whether they recorded it
- **the field solved with the part-cell above the hoops designable (problem 5c64a12f)** — the grid rounds up 1.4 mm past the hoops' top and the optimiser filled that layer over every hoop (a deeper hoop is a stiffer one): the drawn hoops stood 4.8 mm tall against C13's 4 mm limit then, and C13's gate, reading the height from the parameters, passed it. Cells above the guards are now void, the drawing is capped there, and C13 measures the bands as printed — infeasible · source: input topopt3d-petg-12g-settled-5c64a12f7f
- **a 120-iteration field without the settle** — on problem 5c64a12f the objective was still swinging between 41.5 and 51.1 at a 0.1 move limit; 40 damped iterations from it settled at 13.6 with the same layout, so every field is settled the same way — dominated · source: input topopt3d-petg-12g-5c64a12f7f
- **a 3 mm density filter** — on the 2 mm grid it broke the field into one-cell members that the surface blur erased, leaving seats on strands; 5 mm gives limbs at least two cells wide there (the 1.33 mm refinement uses 3.5 mm, 2.6 cells) — dominated
- **void stiffness 1e-3 of solid** — the empty cells carried load between the seats and the hoops, so the optimiser left seats floating with nothing joining them; 1e-6 with direct solves — infeasible
- **the field cut at its midpoint, iso 0.5** — half-dense limbs were cut away and the frame fell into 9 separate pieces; iso 0.36 keeps one piece with the same budget — infeasible
- **a 1 mm grid** — ~840k unknowns: MKL PARDISO's factors took 18 GB and ran the 21 GB machine out of memory, and SuperLU or multigrid would take hours an iteration; 1.33 mm (~360k, 7 GB, 30 s an iteration) refines instead — infeasible
- **the full downwash cost on every cell under a prop** — a deep web under a prop paid the downwash cost for every cell of its depth, though it casts one thin shadow; with 12 mm of depth free the optimiser stayed shallow, objective 32.5 at iteration 89 against 11.4 with the extra shared down each column — dominated
- **the field drawn with a 0.7 mm blur** — it erased limbs one cell thick (the thin flanges, the diagonal staircases) and left stubs the author circled; CalculiX found the drawn seat 1.25 N/mm against the optimiser's 15.3, and at 0.35 mm 15.7 — dominated
- **the 2 mm field, drawn as it is** — its thinnest limbs are one cell wide, grey, and draw as beads; refined at 1.33 mm they are two to three cells thick and continuous (objective 9.97 -> 9.12) — dominated · source: input topopt3d-petg-deep2-ebd95b68bf
